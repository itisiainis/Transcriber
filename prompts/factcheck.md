Fact-check this video.

Pull out every checkable factual claim — figures, dates, attributed quotes,
named studies, statements about what an organisation or person did. Then use
web search to verify each one against a primary source where you can.

For each claim report: what was claimed, what you found, and a verdict —
correct, misleading, outdated, unverifiable, or wrong. Say which source you
checked against. Where a figure is roughly right but framed misleadingly,
say so; that is more common than an outright error.

Check every claim with at least one search before calling it unverified, and
say "could not find a source" rather than "not checked" — the two mean
different things to me.

Flag separately anything presented as established fact that is actually
contested, and anything where the speaker has an obvious incentive to
mislead (selling something, promoting their own channel or service).

Finish with a one-line verdict on whether the video is broadly reliable, then
the two or three stretches worth watching: timestamp range, its length, and a
sentence or two on what is in it and why it is worth the time.
